In preparation

Accept, change or reject?

Emotional intelligence and trust in generative AI · Collaborative study with a Global Banking School colleague · Funding route to be confirmed

The question

Students are told to check what generative AI tells them, but we know much less about who actually does. Over-trust and under-trust are both problems. This study asks whether emotional intelligence, the capacity to notice, understand and manage emotions in oneself and others, is related to how adult business students respond to AI advice, and in particular whether they accept, change or reject advice that contains an error. It is being developed alongside a journal article.

Research questions

  1. Is emotional intelligence associated with how students evaluate, rely on and challenge AI advice?
  2. When AI advice contains an error, is emotional intelligence associated with whether students accept, change or reject it?
  3. Do students who reject flawed advice also reject sound advice; in other words, is this discernment or general distrust?
  4. Do frequency of AI use and prior AI training change these associations?

Approach

An anonymous online survey of adult business students combines an established self-report measure of emotional intelligence with short items on AI engagement and two business scenarios. In each, an AI assistant makes a recommendation: one is sound, the other contains a single checkable error, such as a break-even calculation that uses selling price instead of contribution per unit. Students decide whether to accept, change or reject each recommendation and explain why.

Planned outputs

  • A co-authored journal article
  • A short classroom activity built from the scenarios, for lecturers teaching students to check AI advice
  • A staff session sharing the findings

Key references

  • Lee, J.D. and See, K.A. (2004) ‘Trust in automation: designing for appropriate reliance’, Human Factors, 46(1), pp. 50–80. doi:10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392
  • Glikson, E. and Woolley, A.W. (2020) ‘Human trust in artificial intelligence: review of empirical research’, Academy of Management Annals, 14(2), pp. 627–660. doi:10.5465/annals.2018.0057
