Research

My research asks how generative AI can support, rather than undercut, assessment, feedback and learning design for Level 4 international students.

Doctoral project

PhD in Education (part-time, in progress), Liverpool John Moores University, January 2026 – present

Developing and Evaluating a Lecturer-Fed, Module-Specific AI Agent for Personalised 24/7 Feedback: An Action Research Study with Level 4 International Business Students

Programme approval was ratified in September 2026.

The project grows out of my teaching of international students at Study Group (LJMU International Study Centre) from 2024 to 2026, including two Level 4 modules (Marketing and Business Management; Business Decisions, Strategy and Global Business) and the Level 3 module International Business and Environment, and it is designed as practitioner action research in the modules I teach.

The problem

The problem the project addresses is a mismatch rather than a shortage of tools. Students already turn to generic AI outside class, but its answers are often unaligned with the module’s outcomes and criteria, can be inaccurate, and risk over-reliance at the point where independent judgement should be forming. Detection and bans leave that gap in place and add mistrust.

The agent

The agent uses retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): before it answers, it retrieves from materials I curate for the module, such as the syllabus, lecture notes, rubrics, criteria and exemplars. It is intended to be available 24/7 for content questions, assignment clarification, draft feedback and practice.

It is instructed never to complete assessed work or invent references, and to push students to refine their own argument and to question its guidance.

A prototype has been built and tested on sample module materials, and further testing is under way. Use with students will follow ethical approval. See the innovation showcase.

What the study examines

The study will look at whether an agent of this kind gives accurate, aligned and useful feedback; how students use and value it, including for academic confidence, language support, feedback literacy and independent thinking; how its design changes as I refine it across cycles; and which principles other lecturers could adapt.

Method and ethics

The study is insider practitioner action research, designed as iterative plan–act–observe–reflect cycles in the modules I teach, using surveys, focus groups, anonymised usage logs and a reflexive journal.

Because I am both lecturer and researcher, ethics sit in the design. Student-facing activity will begin once ethical approval is in place. Participation will then be opt-in, with the right to withdraw without effect on marks, and grades will be decided independently of research involvement. No student data have yet been collected.

Equity

Equity is an explicit test. I will examine whether the agent helps or widens gaps for students with different levels of language proficiency and digital access, and I will trial plain-language and simplified explanations.

Contribution

My reading so far suggests that course-grounded AI tools are technically achievable, but that Level 4 business students using a module agent, and insider action research on such an agent, remain thinly studied. I aim to contribute evidence from that setting and design principles that other lecturers can adapt.

I am also attentive to how evaluations are designed: claims about an agent mean little unless method and time are held constant across comparisons.

Research interests

Generative AI and personalised feedback; assessment and learning design; inclusion and international and EAL learners; academic standards, evaluative judgement and feedback literacy; digital pedagogy; practitioner action research.
